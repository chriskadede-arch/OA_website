Our Purpose

Des Bowden, Co-Founder & CEO, Oceans Alive | Co-Founder & Patron, Kuruwitu Conservation

Our Mission

To create, connect and catalyze co-management initiatives by providing support to BMU and Coastal Indigenous People and Local Communities (IPLC), sharing relevant knowledge and building capacity.

The Challenge We Are Addressing

For generations, Kenya's coastal people have relied on the ocean for food and work. Today, this way of life is under threat. Coral reefs are struggling because of climate change, pollution, and overfishing. When the reef suffers, families face hard times, with fewer fish to catch and less money to support their homes.

Due to population growth, more people are now fishing than ever before, and this has caused fish numbers to drop. To catch what they need, some fishers use nets that are illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU). These methods destroy coral and seagrass and catch fish that are too small before they have reproduced, and because there is little help from local authorities to stop these harmful practices, the ocean continues to suffer.

What Actually Works

Conservation thrives when communities are well-organised and work in strong, long-term partnerships. For over 10 years, Oceans Alive has walked alongside coastal communities to provide the technical, institutional, and financial support needed to turn their vision for a healthy ocean into a reality.

Our Vision

Marine conservation of Kenya's coastline and its coastal Indigenous People and Local Communities (IPLC) resource use in balance with nature to support well-being and sustainable livelihoods.

How We Work

Four Core Principles

01
Principle 01

Community Ownership

We believe true conservation is an exercise in local leadership. Our programs are co-designed with fishers, Beach Management Units (BMUs), and local leaders. By placing governance in the hands of those who live by the sea, we ensure the solutions we build together last for generations.

02
Principle 02

Integrated Resilience

A reef cannot thrive if its community is trapped in economic vulnerability. We take an integrated approach, connecting ocean health to land-based prosperity. By pairing reef restoration with homestead gardening and circular economy projects, we ensure that a healthy ocean creates a healthy, stable community.

03
Principle 03

Evidence-Led Action

The tengefu is a living classroom where local wisdom is documented as key data. Our research, conducted by local reef monitors, underpins everything we do. This ensures our work is scientifically rigorous, locally relevant, and capable of informing national policy.

04
Principle 04

Inclusion at the Core

Conservation succeeds when everyone has a seat at the table. Women and youth are central to our work, driving the initiatives that define our impact. Through Trash4Cash, permaculture and alternative income generation programs, we foster economic independence alongside stewardship to build a future that is inclusive and secure for all.
