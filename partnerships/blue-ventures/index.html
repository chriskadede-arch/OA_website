Our Joint Mission

The KAMAMKUKI
Co-Management Area

The KAMAMKUKI JCMA spans approximately 304 km² of coastal and marine waters within Kenya's territorial sea. It brings together the Kuruwitu, Kanamai and Mtwapa BMUs in Kilifi County and the Marina and Kidongo BMUs in Mombasa County.

These institutions work alongside the Kenya Fisheries Service (KeFS) and the respective county governments to manage shared fisheries resources under the framework established by the Fisheries Management and Development Act No. 35 of 2016.

"The project's approach is centred on building the capacity of BMUs and allied co-management institutions to implement fisheries regulations and management measures effectively and accountably."

Two-Phase Implementation

Building Capacity
Step by Step

The project responds to a set of interrelated pressures on the JCMA's fisheries and unfolds in two strategic phases from October 2024 to November 2026.

1

Phase 1: Foundation

Oct 2024 – Sep 2025

Phase 1 focused on diagnosing and addressing governance and institutional capacity gaps across the five BMUs and establishing a fisheries catch-monitoring system at ten landing sites within the JCMA.

Institutional Assessment

Governance and capacity diagnostics across all five BMUs

Catch Monitoring

Ten landing sites equipped with fisheries data collection systems

Baseline Data

Establishing fisheries and ecological baselines for adaptive management

Capacity Gaps Identified

Strategic roadmap for Phase 2 strengthening

2

Phase 2: Strengthening

Dec 2025 – Nov 2026

Phase 2 builds on those foundations across four priority areas: strengthening BMU governance and compliance through a structured mentorship programme; promoting peer learning, knowledge exchange and collaboration among BMUs; improving the financial resilience of fisheries-dependent communities through Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs); and deepening fisheries and ecological monitoring to support evidence-based management decisions.

The Context

Addressing Interrelated
Pressures

The project responds to a set of interrelated pressures on the JCMA's fisheries: destructive and illegal fishing practices, declining fish stocks, habitat degradation, weak enforcement, limited institutional capacity within co-management bodies, and the growing economic vulnerability of coastal communities whose livelihoods depend on increasingly stressed resources.

Destructive Fishing

Illegal and destructive fishing practices degrading marine habitats

Declining Fish Stocks

Overfishing and environmental pressures reducing fish populations

Habitat Degradation

Coral reefs, seagrass beds, and mangroves under increasing stress

Weak Enforcement

Limited capacity to enforce fisheries regulations and management measures

Institutional Gaps

Co-management bodies lack capacity and resources for effective governance

Economic Vulnerability

Coastal communities increasingly dependent on stressed fisheries resources
