Our Story

A Journey of
Community & Ocean

Oceans Alive is a Kenyan marine conservation foundation built on action. We work alongside coastal communities to restore the ocean through hand-planted coral reefs, rebuilt fisheries, and local stewardship.

Our story began in 2003 when a shared concern for the reef brought residents and the Kuruwitu fishers together. What started as a temporary 30-hectare tengefu — a no-take zone — became permanent at the fishermen's own request.

In January 2022, Oceans Alive delivered Kenya's first formal ocean co-management plan — placing 150 km² of sea under community stewardship. From our roots in Kuruwitu, we now accelerate this recovery across the coastline — led by the community, for the community.

2003Founding Milestone

Kenya's First Marine Sanctuary

Desmond Bowden and Kuruwitu's fishermen draw a line in the water. Thirty hectares of reef given permanent protection at the community's own request — a tengefu that would outlast every sceptic.

16
2016

Oceans Alive Takes Form

Thirteen years of proof become an organisation. The Trust is registered with a mandate to carry the Kuruwitu model the full length of Kenya's 640 km coastline.

19
2019Global Recognition

The World Takes Notice

The UNDP Equator Prize lands. In the same breath, hands go into the water — the coral gardening programme begins. Since then, the people who depend on the reef have planted over 25,000 colonies.

22
2022Policy Landmark

150 km² Written Into Law

Kenya's first ocean co-management plan is signed — 150 square kilometres of sea placed under formal community stewardship. Nothing like it had existed before on this coastline.

23
2023Royal Endorsement

A King Kneels at the Reef

His Majesty King Charles III places a coral restoration block bearing his royal cypher into Kuruwitu's waters — a quiet act that carried the community's work to every corner of the world.

25
2025IUCN 2025

A Blueprint for the World

At the IUCN World Conservation Congress, Kuruwitu is presented not as a success story — but as a template. Community-led marine governance, ready to scale across the entire Western Indian Ocean.

Women & Ocean

Women at the Heart of Conservation

From coral restoration divers to eco-entrepreneurs to community leaders, women are at the heart of the Oceans Alive model. The LIFT Network and Trash4Cash programmes place financial independence and conservation leadership directly in the hands of Kilifi's coastal women.

Women collect, sort, and sell plastic waste — transforming environmental harm into economic gain while protecting the beaches and reefs that sustain their communities. Over 80 women now participate, collecting more than 2 tonnes of plastic monthly.
