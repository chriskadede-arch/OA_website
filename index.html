Our Story

Reimagining Marine Conservation through community leadership on Kenya's Coast

Along Kenya's northern coastline, where coral reefs meet ancient forests and generations of fishing families have relied on the sea, a different approach to conservation has been taking shape.

It is a story not only about protecting marine ecosystems, but about redefining who leads conservation, who benefits from it, and how lasting change is created.

Since its founding in 2017, Oceans Alive has grown from the success of community-led conservation efforts in Kuruwitu to become one of Kenya's leading marine conservation organisations. What makes Oceans Alive different is a simple belief: conservation works best when it is built with communities, not for them.

03
2003

A Community Takes Action

Following a devastating coral bleaching event in the late 1990s, fish stocks declined and many fishing families struggled to make a living. In Kuruwitu, community members came together, supported by local resident Des Bowden, to find a way forward.

06
2006Founding

Kenya's First Community Marine Sanctuary

As part of a five-year management plan, the Kuruwitu community set aside 30 hectares of its inner lagoon as a protected area, known locally as a tengefu. What began as a local response to declining fish stocks became the foundation of a community-led approach to marine conservation.

17
2017

Oceans Alive Takes Shape

The Oceans Alive Foundation was established to support and expand the community-led conservation work taking place along Kenya's coast. Building on lessons from Kuruwitu, the organisation helps communities, government and partners work together.

17
2017UNDP Prize

Equator Prize

The Kuruwitu CBO, supported by Oceans Alive, received the UNDP Equator Prize for outstanding community-led marine conservation. Community representatives Katana Ngala and Shida Shoka travelled to New York to accept the award.

22
2022Policy Landmark

120 km² of Ocean Under Co-Management

Years of collaboration led to the formal establishment of the Kuruwitu Co-Managed Area. Covering 120 square kilometres of ocean, it marked a major step forward for community participation in marine governance on Kenya's coast.

23
2023Royal Visit

Royal Recognition

In 2023, during a visit to Kuruwitu, His Majesty King Charles III placed a coral restoration block bearing his royal cypher into the water. The moment brought international attention to a conservation story that began with a local community.

25
2025IUCN 2025

Sharing the Model

At the IUCN World Conservation Congress, the Kuruwitu experience was presented as an example of community-led marine conservation in practice. What began as a local response to a crisis is now informing conversations about marine stewardship across the Western Indian Ocean.

Women & Ocean

Women at the Heart of Conservation

Through Oceans Alive's Social, Health and Environment programme, women are helping shape the future of conservation across Kilifi County.

From coral restoration and community leadership to small businesses and recycling initiatives, women are creating practical solutions that benefit both people and the environment.

Today, more than 120 women collect, sort and sell over two tonnes of plastic each month, turning waste into income while helping protect the coast they call home.
