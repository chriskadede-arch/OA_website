## The 14 Programmes

---

### 01 · Coral Restoration
**Category:** Ecosystem
**Flagship Programme · Partnership: Coral Guardian International**

Since 2019, Oceans Alive has worked with us and partners Coral Guardian to rebuild Kilifi's degraded reefs. Community fishers are trained as certified reef gardeners — growing coral fragments in underwater nurseries and transplanting them to bleached areas. The programme blends world-class marine science with deep local ownership.

**Key Statistics:**
- 24,800 colonies planted
- Founded: 2019
- 40+ community divers trained
- 85% survival rate from nursery to reef

Community fishermen who once extracted from the reef now restore it. Every diver is a trained local; most were fishermen before becoming reef gardeners.

---

### 02 · Community Co-Management (LMMA)
**Category:** Governance

Kenya's first Locally Managed Marine Area (LMMA). 120 km² under a formal management plan signed in 2022, placing communities at the centre of marine governance.

In January 2022, Oceans Alive delivered the first Co-Management Plan in Kenya under new government guidelines — a historic policy landmark. The plan gives coastal fishing communities formal stewardship of 150 km² of ocean, backed by legal framework.

---

### 03 · Project REEL Kilifi
**Category:** Media

Canon CENA and Athlete Media Group with us and partners. World-class photography and film documenting conservation work along the Kilifi coast.

Project REEL captures the human stories behind the science — the reef gardeners, the women's groups, the school children — and shares them with global audiences to build awareness and support for community-led conservation.

---

### 04 · UK-Funded Biodiversity Programme
**Category:** Institutional

UK Government FCDO-funded initiative building institutional capacity across coastal management systems. Strengthening the people and structures that protect Kenya's marine environment for the long term.

This programme invests in the systems, governance frameworks, and human capacity that allow conservation gains to be maintained and scaled beyond the lifetime of individual projects.

---

### 05 · Ocean Education & Youth
**Category:** Education

School programmes, internships and youth engagement. The tengefu as a living classroom, growing the next generation of coastal stewards.

Oceans Alive has reached over 2,500 young people with marine conservation education. Students from Kilifi County schools learn about coral anatomy, reef ecology, fish biology, and the methods their community uses to protect and restore the ocean.

---

### 06 · Kilifi BMU Network
**Category:** Governance

Connecting all Beach Management Units across Kilifi County. Ensuring fishers' voices reach decision-makers at county and national level.

Beach Management Units (BMUs) are the formal governance structures for Kenya's coastal fishing communities. Oceans Alive coordinates and strengthens the network so that grassroots knowledge and concerns translate into real policy change.

---

### 07 · Trash4Cash
**Category:** Livelihoods

Women's groups collect and sort plastic waste, selling to certified recyclers. Turning ocean pollution into household income and clean coastlines.

Trash4Cash transforms environmental harm into economic opportunity. Women's groups across Kilifi collect, sort, and sell plastic waste — earning income while reducing the plastic pollution that threatens marine ecosystems. Clean coastlines and financial independence go hand in hand.

---

### 08 · Turtle Protection
**Category:** Conservation

Monitoring and protecting sea turtle nesting habitats along the Kilifi coast. Community rangers record nesting events and prevent poaching year-round.

Sea turtles are critically important to reef ecosystems — and deeply threatened. Oceans Alive trains and deploys community rangers to monitor nesting beaches, protect eggs from poaching, and record population data that feeds into national conservation assessments.

---

### 09 · Marine Governance & Policy
**Category:** Policy

Translating community-collected data into national marine policy frameworks. OAF presented findings at IUCN 2025, placing Kilifi's model on the global conservation stage.

The data gathered by community reef monitors, BMU networks, and field researchers does not stop at the local level. Oceans Alive works to convert that evidence into policy — presenting at IUCN 2025 and engaging with Kenya's national marine governance frameworks.

---

### 10 · LIFT Network
**Category:** Livelihoods

Women's livelihoods, financial independence and eco-entrepreneurship. Building resilient coastal communities from the ground up through women's economic agency.

The LIFT Network supports women in coastal fishing communities to develop sustainable income streams beyond fisheries — reducing pressure on fish stocks while building household economic resilience. Women are agents of conservation, not beneficiaries.

---

### 11 · Kitchen Gardens & Food Security
**Category:** Food Security

Climate-smart gardens for coastal fishing families. Reducing dependence on overexploited fish stocks while strengthening household nutrition and resilience.

When families can grow food at home, the pressure on already-stressed fisheries decreases. Kitchen gardens provide nutritional security while supporting the broader conservation goal: healthier fish stocks and healthier communities.

---

### 12 · Capacity Building & Training
**Category:** Training

Marine governance, protected area management and ecological monitoring training. Building the human infrastructure that makes conservation last beyond project cycles.

Conservation is only as durable as the people who carry it. This programme invests in training community members, BMU leaders, and local staff in the skills needed to manage marine protected areas, conduct ecological surveys, and lead conservation programmes independently.

---

### 13 · Community Health & Wellbeing
**Category:** Wellbeing

Holistic programmes that link ocean health directly to community health — because the wellbeing of Kilifi's people and the health of its waters are inseparable.

A community that is food secure, economically stable, and healthy is a community that can sustain long-term conservation. This programme addresses the human dimensions of coastal resilience — recognising that marine conservation and community wellbeing are not separate goals.

---

### 14 · Research & Monitoring
**Category:** Science

Ecological baselines, fish biomass surveys and coral cover assessments. The evidence base that underpins every programme and every policy recommendation OAF makes.

Rigorous monitoring separates conservation claims from conservation facts. Oceans Alive maintains an ongoing research programme — tracking fish biomass, coral cover, species diversity, and ecosystem recovery over time. This data informs programme design, validates outcomes, and builds the evidence base for national and international advocacy.

## Why It Works — The Tengefu Model

> *"Conservation that works begins and ends with the communities who call the ocean home."*

### The Principle

In Kilifi, the word **tengefu** describes a protected space that the whole community guards. Our programme design starts from that principle: when conservation is imposed from outside, it fails. When it grows from within, it endures.

Across fourteen programmes, you will find the same pattern: community knowledge at the centre, external expertise in service of local goals, and every activity designed so that the people of Kilifi are the primary beneficiaries of a healthy ocean.

### Four Guiding Principles

**1. Community Ownership**
Every programme is co-designed with fishing communities, BMUs and local leaders. Ownership drives the accountability that makes outcomes last.

**2. Integrated Impact**
Restoring a reef only works if the community has other income sources. Teaching children only works if their parents have secure livelihoods. Our programmes are deliberately interconnected.

**3. Evidence-Led Action**
Research and monitoring underpin every programme. Data collected by community reef monitors informs national policy — closing the loop between science and governance.

**4. Gender at the Core**
Women are agents of conservation, not beneficiaries. LIFT Network, Trash4Cash and kitchen gardens are designed to expand women's economic independence alongside ocean stewardship.
