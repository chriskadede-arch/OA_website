### About / Our Story Section

**Section Tag:** Our Story

**Quote:**
> "We believe the ocean's future depends on the communities whose livelihoods depend on it."

**Body:**
Oceans Alive Foundation is an action-based Kenyan not-for-profit trust. Its aim is to create, promote, facilitate, and accelerate environmental, economic, and socio-cultural change for good on the Kenyan coast — with communities, for communities.

Our story begins in 2003 when founder Desmond Bowden responded to a call from fishermen whose livelihoods were collapsing due to coral bleaching, overfishing, and pollution. Together, they created Kenya's first community-led marine sanctuary at Kuruwitu — just 30 hectares. That sanctuary, at the fishermen's own request, became permanent.

In January 2022, Oceans Alive delivered the first Co-Management Plan in Kenya under new government guidelines — placing 150 km² of ocean under community-led management. What began as 30 hectares is now a 120 km² globally recognised model.

**Founded:** 2003, Kilifi County, Kenya — By Desmond Bowden & the Kuruwitu fishing community. Now operating across the full Kenyan coastline and recognised internationally.

**Award Badge:** UNDP Equator Prize — *Globally recognised for community-led conservation excellence*

### Milestone Timeline — A Journey of Community & Ocean

**Header:**
> From a 30-hectare sanctuary to Kenya's first co-management plan — two decades of measurable, community-led impact along 640 km of coastline.

**Milestones:**

**2003 — Kenya's First Marine Sanctuary** *(Founding Milestone)*
Desmond Bowden and Kuruwitu's fishermen draw a line in the water. Thirty hectares of reef, given permanent protection at the community's own request. A tengefu that would outlast every sceptic.

**2016 — Oceans Alive Takes Form**
Thirteen years of proof become an organisation. The Trust is registered with a mandate to carry the Kuruwitu model the full length of Kenya's 640 km coastline.

**2019 — The World Takes Notice** *(Global Recognition)*
The UNDP Equator Prize lands. In the same breath, hands go into the water — the coral gardening programme begins. Over the next five years, 24,800 colonies will be planted by the people who depend on them most.

**2022 — 150 km² Written Into Law** *(Policy Landmark)*
Kenya's first ocean co-management plan is signed — 150 square kilometres of sea placed under formal community stewardship. Nothing like it had existed before on this coastline.

**2023 — A King Kneels at the Reef** *(Royal Endorsement)*
His Majesty King Charles III places a coral restoration block bearing his royal cypher into Kuruwitu's waters — a quiet act that carried the community's work to every corner of the world.

**2025 — A Blueprint for the World**
At IUCN 2025, the Kuruwitu model is presented as a globally scalable blueprint for marine governance — from 30 hectares to a global vision.

### Editorial Intro — Our Integrated Approach

**Quote:**
> Conservation that works begins and ends with the *communities* who call the ocean home.

**Body:**
At Oceans Alive Foundation, no programme stands alone. Our fourteen initiatives are deliberately interlocked — reef restoration that trains community divers, governance structures that carry fisher voices to national policy, women's livelihoods that reduce pressure on fish stocks, and youth education that plants the next generation of reef guardians.

This is the tengefu model: the Swahili concept of a protected space that belongs to everyone. When communities own their conservation, they defend it. When it provides livelihoods, they sustain it. When it educates their children, they pass it on.

**Four Pillars:**

| Pillar | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Marine Health | Coral, turtles, fish biomass and ecosystem integrity |
| Community Power | Governance, policy, BMU networks and co-management |
| Livelihoods | Women's enterprise, Trash4Cash and food security |
| Knowledge | Education, research, training and documentation |

### Approach — Why It Works

**Headline:** The tengefu model: conservation that belongs to *everyone*

**Body:**
In Kilifi, the word tengefu describes a protected space that the whole community guards. Our programme design starts from that principle: when conservation is imposed from outside, it fails. When it grows from within, it endures.

Across fourteen programmes, you will find the same pattern: community knowledge at the centre, external expertise in service of local goals, and every activity designed so that the people of Kilifi are the primary beneficiaries of a healthy ocean.

**Four Guiding Principles:**

**Principle 01 — Community Ownership**
Every programme is co-designed with fishing communities, BMUs and local leaders. Ownership drives the accountability that makes outcomes last.

**Principle 02 — Integrated Impact**
Restoring a reef only works if the community has other income sources. Teaching children only works if their parents have secure livelihoods. Our programmes are deliberately interconnected.

**Principle 03 — Evidence-Led Action**
Research and monitoring underpin every programme. Data collected by community reef monitors informs national policy — closing the loop between science and governance.

**Principle 04 — Gender at the Core**
Women are agents of conservation, not beneficiaries. LIFT Network, Trash4Cash and kitchen gardens are designed to expand women's economic independence alongside ocean stewardship.
