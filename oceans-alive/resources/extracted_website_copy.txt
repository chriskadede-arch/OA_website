But the prospects of fishing communities in terms of wellbeing and economic empowerment have changed profoundly.Previously (current website):  “… illegal dragnets have proliferated — destroying coral and seagrass habitats and taking increasingly small juvenile fish.”Population growth has driven an exponential increase in the number of artisanal fishers. As fish stocks have dwindled, illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing — including dragnets — has proliferated, destroying coral and seagrass habitats and taking increasingly small juvenile fish. Governance capacity is weak and gear restrictions remain largely unenforced.What actually works: Of some 30 Kenyan communities that set aside voluntary fisheries closures over the past 20 years, only a handful show ecological and social improvement — and in each case success is linked to long-term, place-based community organisation and strong partnership. With 20+ years of engagement in the region, Oceans Alive fills the gap between community effort and the technical, institutional and financial support needed to sustain it.CORE VALUESCommunity-Led · Sustainability · Learning & Education · Conservation in Action · Partnership3.3  Recognition & Awards pageCommunity / KCW-CBO: 2017 UNDP Equator Prize · 2022 IUCN Blue Champions Award.Oceans Alive: 2021 WEF Top Ocean Innovator · IUCN/EU BIOPAMA award · features in IUCN Panorama, Green Listing and the Great Blue Wall.» Editor note: Awards separated by who actually holds them — some belong to the KCW-CBO, not OA.
