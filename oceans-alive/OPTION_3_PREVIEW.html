Our Story

"We believe the ocean's future depends on the communities who's livelihoods depend on it."

Oceans Alive Foundation is an action-based Kenyan besed not for profit. It's ami is to Created, promote, facilitate, and accelerate environmental, economic, and socio-cultural change for good on the Kenyan coast — with communities, for communities.

Our story begins in 2003 when founder Desmond Bowden responded to a call from fishermen whose livelihoods were collapsing due to coral bleaching, overfishing, and pollution. Together, they created Kenya's first community-led marine sanctuary at Kuruwitu — just 30 hectares. That sanctuary, at the fishermen's own request, became permanent.

In January 2022, Oceans Alive delivered the first Co-Management Plan in Kenya under new government guidelines — placing 150 km² of ocean under community-led management. What began as 30 hectares is now a 120 km² globally recognised model.

20 Years of Change

A Journey of Community & Ocean

From a 30-hectare sanctuary to Kenya's first co-management plan — two decades of measurable, community-led impact along 640 km of coastline.

2003
2003/ Kenya's First Marine Sanctuary — Desmond Bowden and Kuruwitu's fishermen draw a line in the water. Thirty hectares of reef, given permanent protection at the community's own request. A tengefu that would outlast every sceptic.
Founding Milestone
2016
2016/ Oceans Alive Takes Form — Thirteen years of proof become an organisation. The Trust is registered with a mandate to carry the Kuruwitu model the full length of Kenya's 640 km coastline.
2019
2019/ The World Takes Notice — The UNDP Equator Prize lands. In the same breath, hands go into the water — the coral gardening programme begins. Over the next five years, 15,000 colonies will be planted by the people who depend on them most.
Global Recognition
2022
2022/ 150 km² Written Into Law — Kenya's first ocean co-management plan is signed — 150 square kilometres of sea placed under formal community stewardship. Nothing like it had existed before on this coastline.
Policy Landmark
2023
2023/ A King Kneels at the Reef — His Majesty King Charles III places a coral restoration block bearing his royal cypher into Kuruwitu's waters — a quiet act that carried the community's work to every corner of the world.
Royal Endorsement
2025
2025/ A Blueprint for the World — At the IUCN World Conservation Congress, Kuruwitu is presented not as a success story — but as a template. Community-led marine governance, ready to scale across the entire Western Indian Ocean.
IUCN 2025

Women & Ocean

Women at the Heart of Conservation

From coral restoration divers to eco-entrepreneurs to community leaders, women are at the heart of the Oceans Alive model. The LIFT Network and Trash4Cash programmes place financial independence and conservation leadership directly in the hands of Kilifi's coastal women.

Women collect, sort, and sell plastic waste, transforming environmental harm into economic gain — while simultaneously protecting the beaches and reefs that sustain their communities.

Fisheries & Livelihoods

Fishing Communities Leading Change

Oceans Alive works with fishing communities to establish rotational fishing zones, gear transition programmes, and community-managed marine areas that rebuild fish stocks while maintaining the food security of coastal families.

Our collaborative management framework brings all key stakeholders together — including coastal communities — under a structure now nationally and internationally recognised as a model for sustainable fisheries governance.

Capacity Building

Building the Next Generation of Ocean Leaders

Oceans Alive's internship and student placement programmes bring aspiring marine conservationists from Kenya and around the world to learn real conservation in a real ocean — building capacity that lasts far beyond any single project or funder.

Our training programmes in marine governance, protected area management, and ecological monitoring are building the long-term institutional capacity that sustains conservation across Kenya's coast.
