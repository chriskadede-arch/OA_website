With funding provided by the EU through BIOPAMA, Oceans Alive has procured a patrol boat for the Kuruwitu Beach Management Unit (BMU). The boat is used for patrols within the expansive 12,000 ha Co-Management Area.

## Launch day

The boat's launch took place at a small ceremony held on 21 July 2023 at the Kijangwani landing site. The boat is equipped with a 40-horsepower outboard engine, can accommodate up to 10 patrol guards, and will revolutionise how the BMU operates.

![BMU members, scouts and partners gather for a briefing under a tree at the launch](/images/blog/kuruwitu-patrol-boat/patrol-briefing-under-the-tree.jpg "A briefing with BMU members, scouts and partners at the launch.")

## Tackling illegal fishing

Approximately 40% of fishermen in Kilifi County use illegal fishing methods, including spearguns and monofilament nets. The patrol boat boosts the BMU's capability to conduct patrols and combat such violations within the Co-Management Area (CMA).

The boat recently played exactly this role, enabling the BMU to carry out a patrol within Vuma, a critical ecological area, in response to reports of illegal fishing gear. The boat also provides much-needed support in the event of maritime emergencies.

![Community members gather around the new patrol boat beside the BIOPAMA project banner](/images/blog/kuruwitu-patrol-boat/patrol-boat-and-biopama-banner.jpg "The new Kuruwitu BMU CMA patrol boat.")

> "Kuruwitu BMU has benefited so much from the patrol boat. We have been able to follow up on neighbouring BMUs who were fishing with illegal gear and practising destructive fishing methods. Thanks a lot to Oceans Alive and the BIOPAMA project."
>
> — Ali, Chairman, Kuruwitu BMU
