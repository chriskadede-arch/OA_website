Oceans Alive is working with the Kuruwitu Conservation and Welfare Community-Based Organisation and the Bureni fishermen community to revitalise their Tengefu (Community Conservation Areas).

A **Tengefu** is an area within the marine environment set aside to recover from degradation caused by unsustainable fishing practices and the destruction of coral cover. Degraded marine environments are often characterised by reduced fish catch and unbalanced ecosystems.

The revitalisation aims to support traditional conservation practices and local community livelihoods. Empowered by the ever-growing LIFT Network, supported by The Rockefeller Foundation, the Oceans Alive project supports the development of sustainable management measures based on traditional knowledge, and improved monitoring, control and surveillance of these vital fisheries.

## Indigenous fishing knowledge

Fishing is a major economic activity for communities along the Kenyan coast, and indigenous fishing knowledge plays a major role in the management of marine resources.

Traditional fishing practices relied fully on indigenous knowledge: a systematic body of knowledge adopted by the people through experience gathered over time. The knowledge is owned by the local communities, who apply it in their day-to-day fishing practices.

## Bureni Tengefu

After 16 years of the Bureni Tengefu being out of operation, Oceans Alive has supported the community group to establish the boundary demarcation of their tengefu, through a community participatory process involving local fishermen, conservationists, the Kuruwitu Beach Management Unit, and the Kilifi County Department of Fisheries.

![Bureni fishermen work together on the beach during the tengefu boundary demarcation](/images/blog/tengefu-revival/bureni-tengefu-boundary-marking.jpg "Bureni fishermen at work on the tengefu boundary demarcation.")

Building on the strength of this multi-stakeholder collaboration, we assembled a Technical Working Committee to spearhead action on the revitalisation of the Bureni Tengefu. In our journey so far we have also seen success in raising community awareness of the ongoing work to revive the Tengefu.

We brought in additional local stakeholders, including the County Ward Administrator, the Vipingo Area Chief, the Kilifi County Fisheries Officer, fishermen from neighbouring fishing communities and Vipingo Beach residents. The feedback from fishermen and conservation groups on their readiness to revive the Tengefu only bolstered our approach, fostering further confidence that the Bureni Tengefu will soon be operational once again.

## Kuruwitu Tengefu

The Kuruwitu Tengefu maintains its position as a pioneering Community Conservation Area in Kenya. It is an inspiration to many coastal communities, with Bureni following closely in Kuruwitu's footsteps.

Inspired by Kuruwitu's rich history, and determined to see it continue as a living classroom for community conservation, our project has supported the Kuruwitu Conservation and Welfare Community Based Organisation in securing its 30-hectare tengefu.

![A Kuruwitu Tengefu scout in patrol uniform watches over the reef at low tide](/images/blog/tengefu-revival/kuruwitu-tengefu-scout.jpg "A Kuruwitu Tengefu scout in the new patrol uniform.")

With our sights set on making it a model Tengefu, we ran further patrol training for the Kuruwitu Tengefu Scouts, engaging Kenya Wildlife Service rangers and Kilifi County Department of Fisheries officers. The scouts were also equipped with patrol uniforms and gear.
