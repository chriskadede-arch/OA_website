**Upscaling 15 years of 30 ha LMMA experience to encompass a 12,000 ha fisheries co-management area in Kilifi, Kenya.**

## The Blue Champions Award

The Blue Champions Award is facilitated by the Blue Solutions initiative, which provides a global platform to collate, share and generate knowledge and capacity for sustainable management and equitable governance of our blue planet.

The award, open to participants from all around the world, aims to support outstanding practitioners around the globe who are engaged in creating healthy oceans. It rewards those who believe that exchanging knowledge and good practices is key to accelerating the changes needed for healthy oceans and sustainable development.

![The Blue Champions Award logo](/images/blog/blue-champions-project/blue-champions-award-logo.jpg "The Blue Champions Award, facilitated by the Blue Solutions initiative.")

Oceans Alive Foundation was proud to be announced as a 2021 winner of the Blue Champions Award, to upscale our solution: 15 years of a 30 ha LMMA experience, grown to encompass a 10,000 ha fisheries co-management area in Kilifi, Kenya. Winning the award helped us to scale up our blue solution.

## How we scaled up

The upscaling of this experience came through:

- The development of the KBMU implementation matrix
- Development of the annual work plan
- Training to build the BMU's internal capacity, with a focus on:
    - BMU orientation on co-management, including leadership
    - Financial management
    - Fish management (in collaboration with Fisheries)
    - Finalising the by-laws and MOU for KBMU and KCW-CBO
    - Enhancing the internal capacity of KCW-CBO
    - Developing the checklist and orienting the BMU and KCW on it

![An Oceans Alive facilitator leads a peer training session with BMU members](/images/blog/blue-champions-project/bmu-peer-training-session.jpg "Peer-to-peer training with Kuruwitu BMU members.")

## Why this approach matters

We at Oceans Alive see this approach and project as vital in light of the drafting and finalisation of the Kuruwitu Co-Management Plan. There is a need, and a requirement, to build on this state-recognised document and put it into action.

We chose this approach because, during the drafting of the Co-Management Plan with the Kuruwitu BMU, the BMU themselves raised institutional capacity as a major issue. We recognise the issue, and see that through the Blue Champions Project, our field experience working with the community, and our six policy principles approach to Community-Based Natural Resource Management (CBNRM), Oceans Alive is well suited to help fill these gaps and take the next steps to operationalise the plan and by-laws.

## Project goals

The Blue Champions Project ran from July 2021 to October 2021, with these goals:

- Share solutions for upscaling to the 12,000 ha CMA
- Develop the KCW-CBO 6 Stage Toolkit for BMUs
- Peer-to-peer training of the Kuruwitu BMU on operational tools
- Develop the operational tools for the by-laws
- Build on the BMU's work in developing the plan, and support its transition to the operationalisation stage

## In line with national goals

The Government has mandated Kilifi County and State Fisheries to put Co-Management Areas (CMAs) in place throughout the county, so this project is in line with their goal. Other initiatives, such as the Blue Economy and World Bank support for marine management, back the inclusion of CMAs along the Kenyan coast as conservation measures that balance advances in fishing technology and maintain sustainability.

Together with the peer-to-peer approach, which Oceans Alive sees as vital, these promote the exchange of this knowledge with surrounding and incoming peers for the future, even in transition periods.
